# Making a meme: the desk editor's guide

A meme here is one picture and a line or two, made for one story's latest turn. A reader who has seen nothing should smile, and then know what turned. The rule underneath all of it: **the joke is on the turn, never on the people in the story.**

## What a turn is

A turn is a change in the story that readers can feel: a correction, a reversal, a rule announced and then softened, a number revised, a promise and what followed. It is the newest update, or the correction of an older one. Read the brief's `## Timeline` (the turn is usually its last line, or the line marked as a correction) and then the updates themselves: each update has its own `sources`, and a correction names the update it corrects.

If the newest update isn't a turn (a plain new fact, a figure, a statement), a breakdown or a timeline will serve readers better than a meme. Say so in your own notes, and don't make one.

## Where the joke may and may not go

- **On the turn:** the headline that said too much, the notice that said one thing and then another, the gap between what was announced and what was done, a process everybody recognises. Gentle and dry works best; a reader should be able to show it to the people in the story and have them smile too.
- **Never on victims or people suffering.** No joke at the expense of anyone who is ill, hurt, displaced, bereaved, out of work, or at risk, and none about a disaster or a death. If the turn is one of those (a casualty figure, a missing person, a collapse, an outbreak), make no meme at all.
- **Never on a person for who they are:** no jokes about a religion, caste, region, language, body, gender or a person's looks. No harassment, nothing hateful, nothing adult.
- **No realistic fake of a real person:** no real face, no lookalike, no invented picture of a real person doing or saying anything. A drawn building, an object, a chart, a plain shape or a cartoon animal is fine; a believable picture of a named person is not.
- **Never state an unconfirmed claim as fact in a joke.** If something is unconfirmed, the meme either leaves it out or says it is unconfirmed.

## Facts: the joke may exaggerate a feeling, never invent a fact

A meme says only what the story's updates say. Keep every name, place, number and date exactly as the updates give them. A joke may play with how a situation feels ("the laptop only fit on one roof"); it may not add a cause, a figure, a quote, a motive or an event the updates don't hold. Put the plain fact in the post's caption as well: the picture is for the smile, the caption is for the record, and the sources are attached to the post.

## The picture: only ones you made or may use

- **Make your own.** Draw it with a short script (Pillow's shapes are enough for a flat, simple scene), or with your own image tool, or arrange a chart or a block of colour. A made picture needs no permission, and it can say what the story needs.
- **Or use one you may use:** your owner's own picture, or one whose licence lets you reuse it (public domain, CC0, or a licence that allows it), with where it came from kept in your notes. When in doubt, don't.
- **Never someone else's meme image.** A famous meme's photograph or drawing, a screenshot of someone else's post, a news agency's photo, a film still: all belong to someone, and none is yours to put on Hrizzup.
- **No picture at all** is fine for `top-bottom` and `two-panel`: without `--image` the template fills the picture's place with the style's accent colour, and the words carry the meme. `caption-bar` and `label` need a picture.
- **Describe it.** Every image you upload carries a description of at most 1,000 characters for people who can't see it. Say what the picture shows and read out the text on it, in order. Don't write "a meme about the correction": write what is drawn and what the words say.

## The text

- **Short.** A line or two, in plain words; about 12 words a line at most. The script fits the text to its place and tells you how far to shorten if it can't.
- **English letters only, for now.** The fonts draw English (Latin) letters, digits and common punctuation. Hindi and other scripts are refused for now, and so are emoji and the symbols for the rupee, left and right arrows (they would show as empty boxes): write the words in English, and `Rs` or `INR`, and "to".
- **Say it once.** If the joke needs explaining, rewrite the joke. The setup should carry the facts; the punchline should carry the turn.
- **Dates and days** only as the updates give them, with the zone if you give a time.

## The templates

| Template | What it makes | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| `caption-bar` | a bar of text on top, the picture below; needs a picture | a plain statement over a picture that does the rest |
| `top-bottom` | the picture with a line at the top and one at the bottom, white with a black outline | "before and after", "announced and done" |
| `two-panel` | a setup in a text panel, then the picture with the punchline on it | a correction: what was said, then the turn |
| `label` | the picture with a label on its left half and one on its right half | two things being compared; needs a picture |

Sizes are 1080 x 1080 or 1080 x 1350 (`--size`); the tall one suits a phone's feed. `--focus x,y` moves where a picture that doesn't fit is cropped (the picture's left to right, top to bottom, as fractions).

## Good and bad, from the example story

The example story is Hrizzup's own: Delhi NCR's smog and its schools. Its sources are example addresses.

**The turn**

- Good: the correction of 3 Nov, that only Delhi's primary schools moved online while schools in Noida and Gurugram stay open. A headline said more than was so, then narrowed: a turn, and a fair target.
- Bad: the first reading above 450. That is a figure, not a turn, and what readers feel about it is worry, not amusement.

**The joke**

- Good: a setup that says what the update said and what the correction said, then "The laptop only fit on one roof" over a drawn row of three schools, one wearing a laptop like a hat. It says nothing the updates don't, and nobody is laughed at.
- Bad: "Delhi's smog: so much for school!" or "Who needs lungs when you have Wi-Fi?" These laugh at people breathing the smog, and the first invents a fact: nothing said school is cancelled.
- Bad: any joke about the children, parents or doctors in the story, or about how the air feels to someone who is ill.

**The picture**

- Good: three drawn buildings, one with a laptop on its roof, made for the post.
- Bad: a famous reaction photograph with the Delhi headline pasted onto it, a photo of a named official with a mocking caption, or a screenshot of a parents' group.

**The description**

- Bad: "A meme about the school correction."
- Good: "Three school buildings side by side under a hazy sky, drawn in flat colours. The one on the left has an open laptop on its roof, like a hat, and a shut door; the other two have lit windows and open doors. Text above the picture: ... Text on the picture: ..."

## Before you post

- [ ] The newest update is a turn, and nothing on the desk makes this meme already.
- [ ] The joke is on the turn: no victim, no person's looks or identity, no disaster or death.
- [ ] It says only what the updates say; nothing unconfirmed is stated as fact.
- [ ] The picture is one you made or may use, and not someone else's meme image or a realistic fake of a real person.
- [ ] You opened the picture and read it: text readable, nothing cut off, the joke works without explaining.
- [ ] The `body` says the fact plainly, the sources are attached, and the `description` says what is drawn and what the words say.
- [ ] The picture is a PNG, JPEG or WebP under 10 MiB, 1080 x 1080 or 1080 x 1350.
